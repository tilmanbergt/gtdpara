# Why gtdpara works this way

gtdpara is built around a few convictions. They explain why some things are there and why others are deliberately missing.

## Your files, no lock-in

Every project and area is a folder, and its todos and meetings live in one small text file inside it (`project.txt` or `area.txt`). You can open these files in any text editor, change them by hand, back them up or move them to another tool. The plugin keeps no hidden database that your data depends on. If you stop using gtdpara, your folders, notes and lists are still there and still readable.

## Calm and made for e-ink

A screen full of tasks says, without words, that all of it needs doing soon. gtdpara tries to show less: only what matters today on Daily, only your `#now` todos in focus mode. Lists are paged instead of scrolled, which also suits e-ink.

## Built on what the Supernote already does

gtdpara adds as little of its own as possible and uses the Supernote's own features instead:

- **notes** for everything you write by hand: meeting notes, notes for a todo, project notes
- **templates** from your MyStyle folder as backgrounds for those notes
- **keywords** on note pages, so the Supernote's keyword search finds them
- **links** from todos and meetings to their notes and files, opened with one tap
- **the lasso** to turn handwriting into a todo or meeting

## The whole life of a project

A project in gtdpara has a clear path: you create it, give it a scope, work through its todos and meetings, mark it done, and then **close it out**. Closing out checks that nothing is left open, moves results to the area or to Resources, and can turn all its notes into one PDF with a table of contents - a record you can keep and read anywhere, also away from the Supernote. Then the folder moves into the Archive, sorted by year. See [Closing out a project](close-out.md).

## Keeping your word clean

gtdpara is inspired by Werner Erhard's and Michael Jensen's work on integrity, in particular their paper [Creating Leaders: An Ontological/Phenomenological Model](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=1681682) (with Kari Granger). It names four foundations of being a leader: integrity, authenticity, being committed to something bigger than oneself, and being cause in the matter.

For a task list, integrity is the most practical of the four: your word is whole when you either keep what you said you would do, or consciously renegotiate it. What breaks integrity is the silent slip - something that quietly stopped being true without you owning it.

That is why gtdpara asks you to say clearly what is **next**, what is in **focus**, and what you are doing **now**. A list of things you might get to is not your word about anything. A few clearly chosen next steps are.

Changing a commitment - moving a due date, taking a project out of focus, putting it on hold - is a real decision, not a typo fix. gtdpara makes these separate, visible actions.

## Room for rest

There are no notifications, no timers and no streaks. When you finish your `#now` todos, nothing suggests the next set. The pause after finishing something is yours. Rest and non-doing are part of a good week, not a gap in it.
